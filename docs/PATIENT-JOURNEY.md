# The patient journey, end to end

One patient, from registration at the front desk to the bill, the certificate and the patient's own
view in the portal. Every step below is exercised **through the real API, in this order, on one new
patient** by `apps/api/test/journey.test.ts` (41 tests; each stage asserts that it sees what the
stage before produced):

```bash
npm test -w apps/api -- journey
```

The table is also the map for walking the same story in the browser. "Where in the app" gives the
route, the menu entry as **Module › Page** (from `apps/web/src/modules.ts`) and the button or tab
label (English source text). Chart tabs are opened with `?tab=<id>` on the chart route
(`/doctor/patients/:id` for doctors, `/nurse/patients/:id` for nurses).

## Gaps in the web app found on the way (all closed)

The API walk and a browser walk of the same story found these breaks in the cycle. Each is fixed,
and `scripts/patient-journey.mjs` now drives the whole cycle through the real pages:

| Gap                                                                                          | Fix                                                                                                                                             |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A checked-in appointment could not be completed by the doctor                                | `/doctor/schedule` offers **Complete** and **No-show** for a checked-in appointment                                                             |
| The arrived patient disappeared from the doctor's home page                                  | **Patients waiting** at the top of the worklist, with **Open chart**                                                                            |
| Registration ended in a dead end for reception (and claimed they joined the care team)       | The success screen offers **Book appointment** for the new patient; the text is correct per role                                                |
| Chart links that nurses could not open (`/doctor/patients/:id` on pages nurses use)          | A doctor-workspace link opens in the nurse's workspace (and the other way round), section included                                              |
| No page for the national medicine catalogue                                                  | `/ministry/catalogue` · Health system › Medicine catalogue: add and edit medicines, ATC code, ingredients and the pack barcode                  |
| Only laboratory staff could mark a specimen as taken                                         | A doctor or nurse of the care team presses **Specimen taken** in the chart (Tests and imaging › Results); the laboratory sees when it was taken |
| Charges appeared up to an hour after the visit (hourly job), so no co-payment at the counter | Opening the patient's account at the front desk captures the charges of the last days first                                                     |
| The pharmacy queue only selected a prescription when the name itself was clicked             | The whole row selects; the detail stays in view next to a long list                                                                             |
| The chart had 35 sections in one row                                                         | Eight areas (Clinical record, Medicines, Tests and imaging, Hospital, Care programmes, Specialist units, Documents, Record and access)          |
| A clinician reading through their unit (ward, radiology, …) could not join the care team     | A short _Temporary access_ note above the chart offers **Join care team**; the summary says whether the reader is a member (`inCareTeam`)       |
| The emergency department did not know when an ambulance would arrive                         | The crew's arrival time is stored; the incoming card shows "Expected 11:49 · in 8 min" (or late)                                                |
| Triage started blank although the crew had measured minutes before                           | The triage form shows the crew's latest observations, early warning score and everything given before hospital                                  |
| After the handover the ambulance record disappeared from view                                | The chart's Emergency tab lists the ambulance calls with the crew's record (without the caller's details); the admitting ward sees it too       |
| The patient could not see an emergency visit or the advice given at departure                | Portal › My record › **Emergency visits**: reason, treating doctor, outcome and the advice; the patient is notified                             |
| The family doctor was not told that their patient attended the emergency department          | The care team is notified on departure (title without clinical detail), linking to the chart's Emergency tab                                    |

```bash
npm run journey
```

needs the app running with demo data (`npm run dev`); it registers a new synthetic patient and
prints one line per step – 46 steps, about three and a half minutes:

1. Front desk registers the patient and books a visit; checks them in on arrival.
2. Doctor sees the patient waiting, joins the care team; records note, diagnosis, allergy and vital
   signs; orders a blood test, prescribes (the allergy check stops amoxicillin) and orders a chest
   X-ray.
3. Ward nurse takes the sample; the laboratory receives it and enters the result, a second
   technician validates it; the doctor acknowledges and releases it.
4. Radiologist performs the X-ray, writes and signs the report; the doctor finds it on the
   worklist, acknowledges and releases it.
5. Pharmacist verifies, receives stock and dispenses; the nurse gives a flu vaccine; the doctor
   completes the appointment.
6. Doctor issues a sick-leave certificate; an employer checks it on the public page (valid,
   initials only).
7. Doctor refers the patient to Prizren; the receiving doctor cannot open the record until they
   accept, then opens it.
8. Doctor admits the patient; the nurse finds them on the bed board, prints the wristband and gives
   a dose by scanning wristband and pack – a wrong pack (amoxicillin) is stopped; the doctor
   discharges with a signed summary.
9. Doctor shares a letter with the patient and asks the nurses for a follow-up call; the nurse
   completes the task.
10. Front desk takes the co-payment and gives a receipt.
11. The patient activates the portal and finds the visit, result, X-ray report, appointment,
    vaccination, certificate, referral, stay, letter, bill and who viewed the record; writes to
    the doctor, who answers, and reads the answer.

The emergency path follows:

12. A man collapses in the street: the ambulance service takes the 112 call and dispatches; the
    crew records observations and aspirin given and leaves for hospital with an arrival time.
13. The emergency department sees the ambulance coming, with its early warning score and expected
    arrival; the crew arrives and the emergency nurse takes the handover of the unidentified man
    (a temporary record); the crew closes the call, which frees the ambulance.
14. Triage shows what the crew measured and gave; the emergency doctor treats him – the chart
    keeps the ambulance record – and admits him to a ward; a nurse confirms his identity when the
    family arrives.
15. The patient of the first part comes back with a cut hand: the front desk registers the
    arrival, the nurse triages, another emergency doctor sutures and sends them home with advice.
    The family doctor is notified, and the patient finds the visit and the advice in the portal.

`--shots <dir>` saves a screenshot after every step.

## Defects the journey exposed in the API (fixed, covered by the journey test)

- **Care-team notifications sent nurses to the doctor's chart route.** A notice to the whole care
  team (correction requests, documents, death certificates, identity changes, …) carried
  `/doctor/patients/:id`, which refuses nurses. `notifyUsers` now writes each recipient's link for
  their own workspace (`linkForRole` in `modules/notifications/notifications.service.ts`).
  Covered by stage _s_ ("every notification link opens in the recipient's own workspace").
- **A sepsis prompt outlived the hospital stay.** After discharge an unanswered prompt stayed on
  the _Sepsis_ board for 24 hours and, an hour later, the worker sent "Sepsis screen not done after
  one hour – {ward}, bed {bed}" for a bed that may already hold another patient. Board and worker
  now only consider patients who are still admitted or in the emergency department of that facility
  (`stillInCare` in `modules/sepsis/sepsis.service.ts`). Covered by stage _k_.
- **A pack barcode could never be registered.** `medication_catalog.barcode` (and `ingredients`)
  could only be set by the demo seed, so outside the demo no pack scan could ever be confirmed and
  every bedside dose needed an override reason. `POST` / `PATCH /stock/catalog` now accept
  `barcode` (a GTIN with a valid check digit, unique) and `ingredients`. Covered by stages _g_ and
  _j_ (wristband + pack scanned; the wrong pack stops the dose).

## Notes

- Direct database access in the journey test is limited to: seeding the drug-interaction reference
  data (a deployment seed), reading audit and notification rows to check what was recorded, and
  running the sepsis escalation worker once (workers have no API).
- The story uses a direct (elective) admission. The emergency-department path (arrival, triage,
  treatment, admission from the ED) is covered by `test/ed.test.ts` and `test/inpatient.test.ts`.
- The facility "vaccinations due" list is the childhood calendar; its updating when a dose is
  recorded is covered by `test/vaccination.test.ts`, not by this adult patient.
